Almost every complaint about dictation accuracy that reaches us turns out, on investigation, to be a microphone problem.

The software gets blamed because the software is the thing you can see. But speech recognition is only ever as good as the audio it receives, and the gap between a laptop's built-in microphone and a properly engineered dictation microphone is not marginal. It is the difference between correcting every third sentence and correcting almost nothing.

This is a guide to what actually matters when choosing a microphone for dictation, and which units are worth the money.

## Why the Microphone Matters More Than People Expect

A built-in laptop microphone is designed for video calls. Its job is to pick up a room and make a human voice intelligible to another human. Humans are extraordinarily good at filtering noise and reconstructing partially heard words from context. Speech recognition engines are much less forgiving.

Three things degrade recognition accuracy, and a good microphone addresses all three.

**Distance.** Signal strength falls off sharply with distance from the source. A microphone 60cm away on a laptop lid is receiving your voice at a fraction of the level a boom microphone 3cm from your mouth receives it, and the noise floor stays constant. The ratio of voice to everything else collapses.

**Ambient noise.** Air conditioning, keyboard noise, traffic, an open-plan office, a fan. A directional microphone with proper noise cancellation rejects sound arriving from the wrong direction. An omnidirectional built-in microphone accepts all of it equally.

**Frequency response and sample rate.** Speech recognition depends on the consonants — the sibilants and plosives that distinguish similar-sounding words. Cheap microphones roll off the high frequencies where those distinctions live. The vowels survive; the consonants blur.

Fix the microphone and a great deal of what looked like a software accuracy problem simply disappears. This holds true whichever [speech to text software](https://www.speechrecognition.cloud) you are running.

## Practical Setup Advice

Whatever you buy, a few things make a disproportionate difference.

**Use USB, not the 3.5mm jack.** A USB microphone carries its own soundcard and bypasses your computer's audio circuitry entirely. Laptop audio circuits sit next to a great deal of electrical noise. Every microphone we recommend above is USB for this reason.

**Position it consistently.** With a boom, keep it to the side of your mouth rather than directly in front — plosives on 'p' and 'b' sounds distort when you breathe straight into the capsule. With a desktop microphone, decide on a position and leave it there. Consistency helps more than perfection.

**Deal with the room before you blame anything else.** Hard surfaces reflect sound and muddy the signal. A rug, curtains or a bookcase behind you will do more for accuracy than an upgrade from a good microphone to a slightly better one.

**Test with your actual work.** Dictate a real document, not a test sentence. The vocabulary you actually use is the only benchmark that means anything.
